## The identification decides the job

The cockroach that matters in Whatcom County is the German cockroach, and the
treatment only works if it is aimed at where that species actually lives.
Getting this wrong wastes months.

**German cockroach.** Small, light brown, with two dark stripes running down the
shield behind the head. This is the one that matters. It lives entirely indoors,
it breeds fast, it concentrates in kitchens and bathrooms, and it moves between
units in multi-family buildings. A German cockroach infestation is a genuine
infestation and it will not resolve on its own. It is not a crawlspace or
moisture insect: it arrives in appliances, cardboard, groceries and used
furniture, and it stays with people.

**American cockroach.** Large, reddish-brown, associated with sewers, drains and
commercial floor drains. Present here but far less common than in warmer parts of
the country, and mostly a commercial issue.

The practical point: a small striped cockroach in your kitchen is an urgent
problem that needs baiting. A cockroach found only in a crawlspace, basement or
outdoors is worth a clear photograph before anyone treats anything, because
German cockroaches live indoors.

## German cockroaches, and why they are difficult

Three things make this species harder than most household insects, and they are
worth understanding because they explain everything about the treatment.

**Reproduction.** A female carries her egg case attached to her body until close
to hatching, which means the eggs travel with her and are protected from most of
what we can apply. Each case holds a substantial number of nymphs, and a
population compounds quickly. This is why a few insects in month one becomes a
serious problem by month four.

**Resistance.** Widespread resistance to the pyrethroid insecticides that make up
most consumer sprays is well documented in this species. That is not a marketing
claim to sell you a service — it is the reason the can you bought did not work
and could not have.

**Behavior.** They are thigmotactic, meaning they seek tight contact on multiple
sides, so they live in cracks, voids, and the inside of appliances rather than in
the open. What you see is a small fraction of what is present, and by the time
you are seeing them in daylight the population is large.

## Why spraying makes it worse

This is the single most useful thing on this page.

Consumer cockroach sprays are contact killers and **repellents**. They do two
things: they kill the insects they directly touch, and they make the treated
surface unpleasant, so everything else avoids it.

Against a German cockroach population that means the survivors scatter — deeper
into wall voids, into adjacent rooms, and in an apartment building, into the
neighboring unit. A population that was concentrated in a kitchen and reachable
becomes a population distributed through the structure and much harder to reach.
People frequently call us after a month of spraying and the job is measurably
larger than it would have been.

Foggers and bug bombs are the worst version of this. They do not penetrate the
cracks where the insects actually live, and the repellent effect drives them
outward.

**What works is bait.** A non-repellent gel that insects feed on and carry back,
with secondary transfer through the population via their droppings and by
cannibalism of dead individuals. It reaches the ones you never see, which is the
whole point. It requires placement in the right locations, in the right
quantities, and it requires not spraying repellents over the top of it — which is
why we ask people to stop treating before we arrive.

Bait aversion is real, so we rotate active ingredients rather than using the same
product indefinitely.

## Where they actually live

On a German cockroach job the treatment is placement, so knowing where they
harbor is the work.

Kitchens dominate: the motor housing and underside of the refrigerator, under and
behind the dishwasher, the void beside and beneath cabinets, cabinet hinges and
corners, around the sink and its plumbing penetration, inside the microwave
housing, and the seams of small appliances — a toaster or coffee maker can hold a
population on its own.

Bathrooms second: under the sink, around plumbing penetrations, behind the
toilet, and inside vanity voids.

Then the structural routes: wall voids around plumbing and electrical, the space
behind switch and outlet plates, and in a multi-unit building, the shared chases
that connect apartments vertically and horizontally.

Cardboard is worth naming separately. Corrugated cardboard is close to ideal
cockroach harborage — tight, dark, and edible — and stacked boxes in a pantry or
a garage frequently hold more of the population than the kitchen does.

## Apartments, and the unit boundary problem

The most common reason a German cockroach job fails is that the scope was one
apartment.

They travel through wall voids, plumbing chases and electrical runs, so the unit
that complains is often not where the population is centered. Treating it while
the units above, below and either side go untouched pushes the insects sideways
and produces a complaint from the next apartment a few weeks later, then back
again.

For a serious multifamily infestation the honest scope includes inspecting the
adjacent units and usually treating them. It is a larger number up front and a
much smaller one over the following year.

**On who pays:** Washington's landlord-tenant law, RCW 59.18.060, generally puts
responsibility for pest control on the landlord in multi-unit buildings, with a
carve-out for single-family residences and for infestations the tenant caused.
Where the answer depends on which side of that you are, we will say so plainly.

## Sanitation, honestly

There is a version of this conversation that is really about blame, and we would
rather not have it.

Cockroaches arrive in deliveries, cardboard, groceries, used appliances,
secondhand furniture and through shared walls. Nobody invites them, and we see
them in immaculate homes regularly.

What sanitation genuinely affects is **how fast a population grows and how well
bait competes**. A kitchen with abundant alternative food gives insects less
reason to take a bait placement, so the two things that help most are removing
competing food and reducing harborage.

Practically: clean under and behind appliances, particularly the refrigerator
motor housing. Keep food in sealed containers. Deal with grease around cooking
surfaces and behind the range. Do not leave pet food out overnight. Take
cardboard out of the building rather than storing it. Fix dripping taps and slow
drains, because they need water more urgently than food. And empty and rinse bins
rather than letting residue accumulate.

None of that is a substitute for treatment. All of it makes the treatment work
faster.

## The health side

Worth being accurate about, because this is one of the few household pests with a
genuinely well-documented health effect.

The main one is **allergens**. Cockroach proteins accumulate in household dust and
are a recognized asthma trigger, with the strongest evidence in children in
multi-unit housing.
[UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/cockroaches/) names the same
allergen and asthma link in its cockroach guidance. That is a better reason to
resolve an infestation properly than the contamination angle people usually reach
for.

They also mechanically carry bacteria across surfaces, having traveled through
drains and voids, so food-contact surfaces in an infested kitchen warrant more
care than usual.

What they do not do is bite, sting, or pose an acute danger to a household.

## How we work a cockroach job

**Identify.** Confirm what it is and where it is breeding. Everything downstream
depends on it.

**Find the harborage.** Appliance voids, cabinet corners, plumbing penetrations,
and in multi-unit buildings the shared routes. Monitors placed where activity is
suspected tell us where the population actually is rather than where it was seen.

**Bait, correctly placed.** Small placements in many locations rather than large
ones in a few, in the tight spaces they actually use.

**Growth regulator** where warranted, to break the reproductive cycle rather than
only reducing adults.

**No repellent sprays over bait.** It is self-defeating, and we will ask you to
stop treating before we start.

**Deal with conditions.** Harborage reduction, moisture, and the sanitation items
above, in writing.

**Follow up.** More than one visit, because of the egg cases. Monitors tell us
whether the population is falling rather than relying on whether anyone has seen
one lately.

## Before we arrive

**Stop spraying.** Repellent products scatter the population and interfere with
bait, and they make the job longer.

**Do not throw out appliances yet.** A refrigerator or microwave can be treated.
Moving an infested appliance out through a building is how a problem spreads.

**Leave a sample if you can.** A cockroach in a sealed bag or a clear photograph
settles the identification immediately, and the identification decides the
whole job.

**Note where and when.** Which room, which time of day, and roughly how many.
Daylight sightings indicate a larger population than night sightings.

**Reduce cardboard** if you can, particularly stacked boxes in pantries and
garages.

## How long it actually takes

Setting expectations, because this is a service where impatience causes people to
do counterproductive things.

**Week one.** Bait placed. Activity often appears to increase briefly as insects
move to feed, which is the treatment working rather than failing.

**Weeks two and three.** Visible activity drops substantially. This is the point
at which people conclude it is finished and stop cooperating with the plan.

**Weeks three to six.** The egg cases carried through the first treatment hatch.
Nymphs appear. This is the expected second act and it is why the follow-up exists,
not evidence that anything went wrong.

**Weeks six to ten.** With the second generation dealt with, monitors go quiet
and stay quiet.

Confirmation is monitors reading zero over consecutive checks rather than
somebody not having seen one lately. In a multi-unit building, add time — the
population is larger and the scope is the building.

If someone tells you a German cockroach infestation is a one-visit job, that is
the clearest signal available about the rest of what they will tell you.

## Commercial and food handling

For restaurants, groceries, care facilities and any business under a food safety
program, cockroaches are a compliance problem as much as a pest one.

The entry route is almost always **deliveries in corrugated cardboard**, which is
why the most effective single intervention at most commercial sites is
de-boxing at the dock rather than carrying cartons into storage. It costs
nothing and it removes the main vector.

The pressure points are the same everywhere: dish areas and the voids behind
them, motor housings on refrigeration, the space beneath and behind cooking
equipment, floor drains, dry storage, and any warm void near a compressor.

For these accounts the record matters as much as the result — what was found,
where, what was done, and what remains on the operator's side. That is part of
our [commercial programs](/commercial/) rather than an extra.

## What people get wrong

Two mistakes account for most of the jobs that drag on.

**Treating the outside of the building.** Perimeter spraying the outside of a
building while a population breeds in the kitchen voids. The population compounds
while the treatment happens somewhere irrelevant, and repellent product around
the structure does nothing to a species that never goes outdoors.

**Blaming the crawlspace.** German cockroaches are not a crawlspace or moisture
insect, so treating or drying the crawlspace does nothing for a population
breeding in the kitchen. The work belongs where they live: appliance voids,
cabinets, plumbing penetrations and shared walls.

The identification takes a minute and a photograph usually settles it.

## The nymphs are the tell

If you want to know whether you have a breeding population rather than a stray,
look for the small ones.

Adult cockroaches wander, arrive in bags and boxes, and occasionally turn up
somewhere that has no infestation at all. **Nymphs do not travel.** They are
small, wingless versions of the adult, and finding them means the population is
reproducing where you found them.

Egg cases are the other confirmation — small, ridged, purse-shaped capsules,
usually tucked into a crack or glued behind an appliance. German cockroach cases
are carried by the female until nearly hatching, so finding shed cases in a
harborage area indicates an established population rather than a visitor.

So the useful report to give us is not just "I saw a cockroach" but whether they
were all full-sized or whether there were small ones too. It changes the scope of
what we look for.
