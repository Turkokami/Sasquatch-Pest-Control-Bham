## What you are actually looking at

Somebody opens a sliding window in late summer and finds the channel packed
with dry grass, cut to length and stuffed in tight. Sometimes there is a
green cricket in there too, not moving. It looks like something went badly
wrong in the window frame.

Nothing went wrong. A single female wasp built a nursery in the most
convenient tube she could find, and the tube happened to be your window.

## The nest, and why the window

Grass-carrying wasps do not excavate. They take over a cavity that already
exists — a hollow plant stem, an old beetle tunnel, a nail hole, a weep hole,
a gap beneath siding, and very often the track of a sliding window or door.
Extension guidance from Ohio State names window wells, weep holes and gaps
beneath siding in the same list, which is a fair description of the average
house.

The grass is the structural material. She cuts long blades, carries them in
with her mandibles, and packs them into the cavity as bedding and as a plug.
The strands sticking out of the opening are simply the ends she could not get
all the way in.

## She is not defending anything

This is the part worth saying plainly, because the shape of a large
thread-waisted wasp near a window sets off the same alarm a yellowjacket does,
and the two could hardly be less alike in temperament.

A yellowjacket nest is a colony with hundreds of workers whose job includes
defense. A grass-carrying wasp is one female working alone. She has no
daughters to call, nothing to lose by leaving, and a sting whose purpose is
subduing crickets. Extension sources describe the genus as beneficial solitary
wasps that rarely, if ever, sting.

## Why we usually tell you to leave it

Ohio State's guidance says management is not required, and that the contents
of a window well can be cleared if they are unsightly or interfere with
opening a window. That is our answer too, and it is the honest one: this is a
cleaning job and, if you want it not to recur, a sealing job.

Where we are useful is the second half. Clearing a track takes a minute.
Working out which cavity she used and closing it — screening a weep hole,
sealing a gap under siding, capping an open tube — is exclusion, and it is the
only thing that stops next August looking the same.
