## What Are Yellow Jackets?

Yellow jackets are social wasps that live in large colonies, sometimes consisting of thousands of members. Unlike honeybees, which die after stinging once, yellow jackets have smooth stingers and can attack multiple times. They are particularly aggressive in late summer when their colonies reach peak numbers and food sources become scarce.

The yellow jackets you are most likely to run into in western Washington are:

- **Western yellow jacket (Vespula pensylvanica)** – A notorious scavenger, often found around garbage bins and picnic areas, and the usual culprit at a late-summer barbecue.
- **German yellow jacket (Vespula germanica)** – An introduced species that frequently builds nests inside walls and other voids.
- **Aerial yellow jacket (Dolichovespula arenaria)** – Builds an enclosed paper nest in a shrub, tree, or under an eave rather than in the ground.

## Where Do Yellow Jackets Nest?

Yellow jackets construct their nests using chewed wood fibers mixed with saliva, creating a paper-like structure. These nests can be found:

- Underground in old rodent burrows
- In trees, shrubs, and bushes
- In attics, eaves, and crawl spaces
- Under decks and porches

Each spring, a single queen starts the nest, and by late summer, it can house thousands of aggressive workers. If left unchecked, yellow jacket nests can expand rapidly, increasing the risk of painful stings for homeowners, children, and pets.

## Why Are Yellow Jackets Dangerous?

Unlike honeybees, which pollinate flowers and produce honey, yellow jackets are carnivorous predators that feed on other insects, meats, and sugary foods. Their aggressive nature and ability to sting multiple times make them particularly hazardous. For those allergic to their venom, a single sting can trigger severe reactions, including anaphylaxis.
