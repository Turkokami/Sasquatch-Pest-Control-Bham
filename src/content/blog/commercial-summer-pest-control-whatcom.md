Summer is peak season for commercial pest pressure in Whatcom County, and the stakes for a business are higher than mere nuisance. A single pest sighting can mean a failed health inspection, a bad review, or a code violation. Warm weather drives ants, flies, wasps, rodents, and stored-product pests toward commercial buildings all at once. The businesses that stay clean through summer are the ones running proactive, scheduled pest management rather than reacting after a customer or inspector spots something.

## Why does summer hit commercial properties so hard?

Warmth accelerates almost everything in the pest world. Insects breed faster and stay active longer, dormant colonies explode in size, and animals that were content outdoors start pushing toward the food, water, and shelter a commercial building offers. In Whatcom County, our mild, damp summers add the humidity many pests love.

Meanwhile, commercial properties present exactly what pests are looking for: dumpsters and food waste, frequent deliveries that carry pests in, propped-open doors and loading bays, and complex buildings with countless entry points. The result is that summer concentrates pressure on the businesses least able to afford a pest problem.

## What pests should commercial properties watch for in summer?

Different property types face different threats, but these are the summer heavy-hitters across Whatcom County commercial buildings:

- **Ants**, which forage in enormous numbers and readily invade break rooms, kitchens, and any space with accessible food or moisture.
- **Flies**, drawn to dumpsters, drains, and food areas — a major concern for restaurants and food handlers, where they are both a contamination risk and an inspection red flag.
- **Wasps, hornets, and yellowjackets**, which build nests around entrances, eaves, signage, and outdoor seating and grow aggressive as summer wears on, posing a liability risk to customers and staff.
- **Rodents**, which stay active year-round but exploit summer deliveries, open doors, and outdoor food to establish themselves before fall pushes them fully indoors.
- **Stored-product pests** in warehouses, groceries, and food-processing facilities, where warm conditions speed up infestations in dry goods.
- **Spiders**, which follow the summer insect boom and become a cosmetic and comfort issue in offices, warehouses, and retail spaces.

## What is actually at risk for my business?

For a commercial property, pests threaten far more than comfort. Food-service and retail businesses face health-code violations, failed inspections, and the reputational damage of a customer spotting a pest — which travels fast on review sites. There is liability exposure when wasps sting a customer. There is product loss in warehouses and groceries when stored-product pests or rodents contaminate inventory. And there is the structural and equipment damage rodents cause over time. For most businesses, the cost of prevention is a rounding error next to the cost of any one of these outcomes.

## What does proactive commercial pest management look like?

The businesses that stay ahead of summer pests treat pest control as ongoing infrastructure, not an emergency call. A strong commercial program generally includes:

1. **A thorough walkthrough** of the building interior, exterior, roof, and grounds to find entry points, harborage, and attractants before they become infestations.
2. **Exclusion and sanitation guidance** — sealing gaps, addressing door sweeps and loading-bay access, and correcting the moisture and waste conditions that draw pests.
3. **Scheduled, recurring service** matched to your property type and risk, so pressure is monitored and treated continuously through peak season rather than after a problem appears.
4. **Targeted treatment** for the specific pests your property faces, applied with commercial operations and customers in mind.
5. **Documentation** — written records of the service performed on each visit, which is what supports your health inspections, audits, and compliance requirements.

## What can my staff do between visits?

Your team is the front line, and a few habits make a real difference. Keep dumpsters closed and positioned away from entrances, and stay on top of the waste and food-debris schedule. Keep exterior doors and loading bays closed when not in use, and check door sweeps and screens. Clean drains and spills promptly, since flies and drain-breeding insects thrive on residue. Store food and dry goods sealed and off the floor.

Most importantly, report early sightings immediately rather than waiting. A single wasp nest or a few ants caught early is a quick fix, while the same problem ignored for two weeks in summer becomes a real infestation.
