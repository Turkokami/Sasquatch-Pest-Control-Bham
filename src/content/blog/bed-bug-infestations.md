## Recognizing Signs of Bed Bugs

**Indicators of an infestation:**

Bed bugs are elusive creatures that often go unnoticed until significant signs appear. Key indicators include:

- **Blood stains:** rust-colored spots on bedding from crushed bed bugs.
- **Fecal marks and shed skins:** look for dark spots (bed bug feces) and translucent exoskeletons in mattress seams, furniture joints, and corners of the room.
- **Live bugs:** adults are flat, oval, and reddish-brown, roughly the size and shape of an apple seed. Younger ones are smaller and paler.
- **Bite symptoms:** bites are often found on exposed skin such as arms, neck, and face, and may appear as raised red spots that become itchy or inflamed. Bites are a prompt to look for physical evidence, not proof on their own — reactions vary enormously from person to person and many people never react at all.

## How Bed Bugs Feed and Behave

**Feeding patterns:**

Bed bugs pierce the skin with elongated beaks to draw blood. Feeding can last up to 10 minutes, often without being noticed by the host. They require blood meals to progress through each of their life stages, and females need them to produce eggs.

**Behavioral traits:**

- **Nocturnal:** most active at night, which is why bites often occur while people sleep.
- **Non-communicable:** unlike mosquitoes, bed bugs are not known to transmit diseases to humans.

## Preventing and Treating Bed Bug Bites

**Self-care for bites:**

If you've been bitten, avoid scratching to prevent infection. Over-the-counter antihistamines and anti-inflammatory creams can relieve itching and swelling. If a rash develops or persists, seek medical attention.
