## What Are Bed Bugs and What Do They Look Like?

Bed bugs are small, parasitic insects that require human or animal blood to survive. While they are not known to transmit diseases, their bites can be highly irritating. Bed bugs are found worldwide and are notoriously difficult to control because of their nocturnal habits and their ability to hide in very small spaces.

Even if you have an infestation, you may not see many bed bugs, since they primarily feed at night and hide during the day. They often live in mattress seams, cracks, and crevices.

### Bed bug life stages

- **Adult bed bugs:** small, oval, and flat with a reddish-brown color, they resemble an apple seed (3/16 to 1/4 inch long). After feeding, they swell and become more elongated.
- **Nymphs:** these young bed bugs are smaller and translucent or whitish-yellow. They can be nearly invisible if they haven't recently fed.
- **Eggs:** bed bug eggs are pearl white and about the size of a pinhead.

## What Do Bed Bug Bites Look Like?

This is where most people go wrong, so it's worth being blunt: **you cannot identify bed bugs from the bites.** Bites are a reason to start looking for evidence, never the evidence itself.

Bed bug bites are often red and slightly swollen, and they sometimes appear in clusters or in a rough line. That line pattern gets repeated everywhere as though it were a signature, and it isn't one. Reactions vary enormously from one person to the next, the same bug feeding on two people in the same bed can produce two completely different results, and a large share of people bitten by bed bugs show no visible reaction at all. Two people can sleep in the same infested room and only one of them wakes up with marks.

The reverse is just as common. Flea bites, mosquito bites, spider bites, scabies, contact dermatitis, and ordinary allergic skin reactions all produce marks that can look exactly like what people picture when they think of bed bug bites. Doctors cannot diagnose bed bugs from skin alone either.

What settles the question is physical evidence in the sleeping area: live bugs, shed skins, eggs, and the dark ink-like fecal spotting along mattress seams and the box spring. If the marks on your skin are the only thing you have, you don't yet know what you're dealing with.

### Symptoms of bed bug bites

- Marks may take up to 14 days to appear, because of the anesthetic and anticoagulant bed bugs inject while feeding.
- Some people never react at all, while others experience redness, swelling, and itching.
- Severe allergic reactions can occur, though they are rare.
- Bites can lead to secondary skin infections if scratched excessively.
- Anxiety and insomnia are common effects of living with an infestation.

## How to Treat Bed Bug Bites

Although bed bug bites typically heal on their own within a couple of weeks, they can be uncomfortable. To relieve symptoms:

- Wash the affected area with soap and water.
- Apply an over-the-counter corticosteroid cream or calamine lotion to reduce itching and inflammation.
- Take an oral antihistamine for symptom relief if needed.
- Consult a doctor if you experience severe reactions, blisters, or signs of infection.

## Preventing Bed Bug Infestations

Since bed bugs spread by hitchhiking on luggage, clothing, and furniture, a few proactive habits go a long way.

### While traveling

- Avoid placing luggage on carpets, beds, or upholstered furniture.
- Check mattress seams, box springs, and bed sheets for blood stains, black specks, or shed skins.
- Look behind headboards, inside dresser drawers, and in sofas and chairs.

### At home

- Check secondhand furniture and household items before bringing them inside.
- Go through luggage and bags outdoors after traveling.
- Vacuum your suitcase or use a hand steamer to kill any bed bugs and eggs.
- Wash and dry all clothes from your trip on hot cycles.

## How to Handle a Bed Bug Infestation

If you suspect bed bugs in your home, look for:

- Live bed bugs in bedding, mattresses, or furniture crevices.
- Shed skins and exoskeletons.
- Dark, rust-colored spots or black specks.
- A sweet, musty odor.
