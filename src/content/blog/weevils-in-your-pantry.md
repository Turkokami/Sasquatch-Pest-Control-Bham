When it comes to household pests, most people think of ants, spiders, or rodents. But lurking in your pantry — right under your nose — could be an even sneakier intruder: the weevil. These little grain-loving pests can turn your stored food into an all-you-can-eat buffet, and before you know it, your flour, rice, or even your spices could be crawling with unwanted guests.

At Sasquatch Pest Control, we know that battling weevils isn't just about clearing out the ones you can see — it's about keeping them out in the first place. Let's break down what these pantry invaders are and how you can protect your home from an infestation.

## Meet the weevils: rice and granary weevils

Two of the most common types of weevils found in stored food are the **rice weevil** and the **granary weevil**.

### Rice weevil (*Sitophilus oryzae*)

- About 1/8″ long, reddish-brown with faint yellowish spots
- Distinct snout with chewing mouthparts
- Can fly and is attracted to light
- Targets corn, wheat, rice, beans, nuts and cereals, and occasionally soft fruit

### Granary weevil (*Sitophilus granarius*)

- Slightly larger at 1/8″ to 1/4″ long
- Dark reddish-brown to black, without spots
- Cannot fly and is not attracted to light
- Infests a variety of stored grains and grain products

Both of these pests have larvae that are small, legless, and white with a dark head. They develop **inside** the individual grain or bean, which is exactly why they are so hard to spot until the adults start emerging. A bag of rice can look perfectly clean and still be carrying the next generation.

## How to spot an infestation

The first sign of a weevil invasion usually isn't a big neon sign saying "hey, your food is infested." Instead, you might notice:

- Small beetles crawling in your pantry or inside food containers, often gathering at the top of a bag or on a nearby windowsill
- Fine, sawdust-like debris in the bottom of a container, from grain hollowed out by the larvae
- Grains or beans with small round exit holes in them
- Grains that float when you tip them into water, because the inside has been eaten out

One useful distinction: **webbing** in a package is not a weevil sign. Silk webbing and clumped, matted food point to a pantry moth such as the Indianmeal moth rather than a weevil. Both get into the same cupboard, but they need slightly different handling, so it's worth knowing which one you're looking at.

If you see any of these signs, it's time to act before the problem spreads to the rest of the shelf.

## Weevil control and prevention

### 1. Store food properly

Weevils are like tiny burglars — if they can't get in, they can't steal your food. Always store dry goods in airtight **glass, plastic, or metal containers** with tight-fitting lids. The original paper or cardboard packaging is no barrier at all, and a weevil that emerges from one infested bag will happily walk into the next one.

### 2. Keep it cool and dry

Heat and humidity help weevils thrive. Keeping your pantry cool and dry slows their reproduction. If you buy bulk grains, flours, or nuts, consider **putting them in the freezer for several days** when you get them home — a deep freeze kills eggs and larvae hiding inside the grain, which is the stage you can't see or sift out.

### 3. Inspect and rotate food

Before bringing dry goods home from the store, look the package over for damage and holes. Once home, use older items first so nothing sits at the back of the shelf long enough to become a breeding site. Weevil problems very often trace back to one forgotten bag.

### 4. Clean your pantry

If you find weevils, remove all infested food and vacuum the shelves thoroughly — especially in the corners and along the shelf edges where eggs or larvae might be hiding. Wipe everything down with warm, soapy water, and **avoid using insecticides** where food is stored. Empty and clean the containers you're planning to reuse, not just the shelf.
