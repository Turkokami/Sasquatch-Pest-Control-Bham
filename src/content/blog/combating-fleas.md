## Identifying flea bites

**Symptoms of flea bites:**

- **On humans:** Flea bites typically appear as small red spots surrounded by reddened haloes, often found on the legs and feet. These bites are extremely itchy and can cause significant discomfort.
- **On pets:** Pets may suffer from flea allergy dermatitis, an allergic reaction to flea saliva, which can lead to excessive scratching, hair loss, and secondary skin infections.

## Understanding flea behavior

**Where fleas come from.** Fleas can jump many times their own height, making it easy for them to move between hosts and environments. They hitch rides on pets, clothing, and other items, and are particularly drawn to warm-blooded mammals. Common sources include other infested animals and outdoor environments like yards or parks.

**Life cycle and habits.** Fleas require blood to reproduce, with female fleas needing a blood meal to produce eggs. They often wait in shady outdoor areas, jumping onto hosts as opportunities arise. Most of a flea population is not the adults you can see — eggs, larvae, and pupae sit in carpet, bedding, and floor cracks, which is why an infestation can appear to rebound days after treatment.

## Preventing flea infestations

**Regular treatments.** To prevent fleas, consistently treat pets with flea prevention products, especially during warmer months or if they frequent areas like dog parks or kennels. Regular grooming and flea treatments for pets are essential steps in managing flea populations at home.

**Home maintenance.** Keep your home clean and free of flea habitats. Vacuum regularly, wash pet bedding frequently, and manage yard areas to reduce flea habitats. Vacuuming matters more than people expect: it removes eggs and larvae from the carpet and prompts pupae to emerge, which is exactly when treatment reaches them.

## Treating flea bites and infestations

**On pets.** Consult a veterinarian before treating your pet. Special shampoos and cool water can ease inflammation, while more persistent symptoms might require prescription medications like steroids or antihistamines.

**On humans.** Wash flea bites with soap and water, apply antiseptic, and use ice packs to reduce itching. Over-the-counter lotions or antihistamines can also help manage symptoms. If bites become infected, seek medical attention promptly.
