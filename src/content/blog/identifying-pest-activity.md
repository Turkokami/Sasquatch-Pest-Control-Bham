## The value of a trained eye

When it comes to keeping your home pest-free, many homeowners rely on their own instincts to spot problems. Rodents and other pests, however, are experts at hiding, and identifying their presence takes a trained eye. At Sasquatch Pest Control, we specialize in locating pest activity in crawl spaces, attics, and attached structures — areas most homeowners rarely go. Professional pest control in Bellingham, WA, and the surrounding area is about catching an infestation early, before it turns into costly damage or a health hazard.

An experienced technician recognizes the signs of an infestation that an untrained eye passes over. Here's what that looks like in practice.

### 1. Identifying conducive conditions

Pests are drawn to certain conditions that make an environment suitable for nesting, breeding, and feeding. A technician looks at:

- **Moisture issues.** Many pests, including termites, ants, and rodents, seek out damp areas. Crawl spaces and attics with excess moisture create ideal conditions for them.
- **Clutter and harborage sites.** Stored items, insulation, and debris provide cover for rodents and insects to establish colonies.
- **Food sources.** Improperly stored food, pet food, and even organic debris in crawl spaces can attract pests.

### 2. Detecting access points

Rodents and pests don't need a wide-open door to enter your home. A gap about a quarter of an inch across — roughly the width of a pencil — is enough for a house mouse, and it is also enough for a rat, because a rat only needs that much of an edge to get its incisors on it and gnaw the hole larger. The extension literature gives rats slightly more than half an inch to pass through, but a building sealed to half an inch is a building a rat chews open, which is why a quarter inch is the working standard for both. Ignore the widely repeated "size of a dime" comparison: a dime is 0.705 inches across, nearly three times a quarter inch, and it is wider than the rat figure it is meant to sit under. Our technicians look at:

- Gaps around vents and utility lines
- Cracks in the foundation or siding
- Roofline openings and soffits
- Poorly sealed doors, windows, and crawl space vents

These hidden access points are one of the biggest reasons infestations persist even after DIY treatments.

### 3. Recognizing the signs of an infestation

Many homeowners miss the early warning signs of an infestation simply because they don't know what to look for. A professional looks for:

- **Droppings and urine stains.** Rodents leave behind evidence of their movement that isn't obvious unless you know where it collects.
- **Gnaw marks.** Electrical wiring, wood structures, and even plastic pipes may be chewed by rodents, raising the risk of fires and leaks.
- **Nesting materials.** Shredded paper, insulation, and dried plant matter indicate an established infestation.
- **Odors and sounds.** Musty or ammonia-like smells, along with scratching noises in walls and ceilings, are common with rodent infestations.

### 4. Determining the right treatment plan

At Sasquatch Pest Control, the approach goes beyond finding the problem — it addresses it. Unlike generic over-the-counter products, a professional treatment plan is built around the specific pest and the specific building. Plans typically include:

- Control using established, current techniques
- Exclusion work to seal entry points and prevent re-entry
- Habitat modification to remove what is attracting the pest
- Ongoing management to keep pressure down through the year

## Why DIY pest control usually isn't enough

DIY solutions can seem like the cheaper route, but they rarely address the root cause of an infestation. Traps, sprays, and bait stations may temporarily reduce visible pest numbers, but they don't reach hidden nests or close the access points that let the pest in. Handling it alone leaves homeowners exposed to:

- Recurring infestations, because the entry points were never sealed
- Health hazards from improper use of pesticides
- Structural damage that worsens quietly over time
- Repeated spending on DIY attempts that don't hold

Working with a professional means the conditions, the access points, and the population all get addressed together rather than one at a time.
