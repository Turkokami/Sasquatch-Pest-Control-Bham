# Phase 4 — the authority layer: why it is blocked, and what unblocks it

Status: **BLOCKED ON THE OWNER.** Not on effort, not on scope, not on a
technical dependency. On facts that only Sasquatch has.

---

## The problem

Phase 4 is fourteen case studies. A case study is an account of a specific job:
this building, this problem, this diagnosis, this work, this outcome.

Every sentence in one is a claim about something that happened.

Nobody writing this site was on any of those jobs. There is no job history in
this repo, no invoices, no photographs, no service records, no notes. The only
way to produce fourteen case studies from here would be to invent them — to
write a plausible account of a 1920s Lettered Streets house with a carpenter
ant colony in a stump, attach a plausible outcome, and publish it as something
that occurred.

That is a fabricated record. It does not become acceptable because the
underlying pest biology is accurate, or because the composite is drawn from
"typical" jobs, or because a disclaimer sits at the bottom. A reader
encountering a case study reasonably takes it as a report of real work. If it
is not, the page is a lie regardless of how carefully the entomology is
researched.

It is also the single worst place on this site to be caught. The entire
positioning of the build — the PENDING sentinel, the evidence classes on
credentials, the refusal to hand-enter a review rating, the pages that talk
customers out of unnecessary work — rests on the site being unusually honest.
Fourteen invented job stories would undo that in one afternoon, and they would
undo it in the section specifically labelled "proof".

So this phase does not get written speculatively and reworked later. It gets
written once, from real material.

## What is needed

For each case study, the following. Rough is fine — this is raw material, not a
form to be filled in neatly.

**Required**

1. **Town or neighborhood.** Not the street address. "A house off Alabama Hill"
   is enough, and is better than an address for the customer's sake.
2. **Building type and rough age.** Post-war rambler, 1910s foursquare,
   1990s two-story, a commercial unit, a barn.
3. **What the customer called about.** Their words if you have them. "Scratching
   over the bedroom." "Ants on the counter every spring for four years."
4. **What it actually turned out to be.** Including where the previous
   diagnosis was wrong, if there was one.
5. **What you did.** The real sequence, including the parts that did not work
   first time.
6. **The outcome, and how you know.** "No activity on the follow-up eight weeks
   later" is a real outcome. "Problem solved" is not.
7. **Roughly when.** Season and year. Season matters more than the year.

**Valuable if available**

8. **What made it difficult** — the crawl access, the neighbor's property, the
   thing nobody could reach.
9. **What you told them not to buy.** These are the strongest case studies on a
   site positioned like this one.
10. **Anything that surprised you.**
11. **Photographs**, with permission. Even phone photos of a crawlspace, an
    entry point or a repair.
12. **Customer permission**, and whether they are willing to be quoted or named.
    Neither is required — an anonymized case study is fine and normal.

**Never needed:** the customer's name, address, or anything identifying, unless
they have explicitly agreed to be named.

## Suggested spread

Fourteen is the target. The value is in covering different *kinds* of job
rather than fourteen versions of the same rodent exclusion. A useful spread:

| # | Kind of job | Why it earns a page |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rodent exclusion on an older crawlspace house | The core service, the most searched, the most commoditized |
| 2 | Carpenter ants where the parent colony was found outdoors | Proves the search-not-spray argument with a real result |
| 3 | Carpenter ants where the parent colony was **not** found | Honesty about a partial result is worth more than a win |
| 4 | A moisture problem misdiagnosed as an insect problem | The "we told them to call a drainage contractor" story |
| 5 | A WDO treatment following somebody else's report | Demonstrates the identify/treat boundary correctly |
| 6 | Bed bugs in a multi-unit or student rental | Scope-by-building, coordination, a hard job done properly |
| 7 | A wasp job where the honest answer was to leave it | Counter-positioning; almost nobody publishes one of these |
| 8 | A ground nest that had already been disturbed | Safety, urgency, real consequence |
| 9 | Commercial — food premises or a shared building | Different constraints, different documentation |
| 10 | A crawlspace restoration that was genuinely warranted | Balances #11 |
| 11 | A crawlspace restoration you talked someone **out of** | The single most valuable page in the set |
| 12 | A job on acreage or a farm property | Whatcom County specificity, feed and outbuildings |
| 13 | A repeat customer whose problem recurred, and why | Nobody publishes these. It would be remarkable if you did |
| 14 | A job you got wrong first time and went back on | Same. This is the one that makes the other thirteen credible |

Numbers 3, 7, 11, 13 and 14 are the ones with real competitive value. Every
pest control site in the county has a version of 1 and 2. None of them has 14.

---

## How to supply it

Whatever is easiest. A voice memo per job is genuinely the best format —
speaking a job through takes four minutes and produces better material than
writing does. A page of notes works. A spreadsheet works. Old invoices with a
sentence of context each work.

Fourteen is the target, not the minimum. Six good ones beat fourteen thin ones,
and the phase can ship in batches.

---

## What has been built ahead of the content

Nothing yet, deliberately — building an empty case-study route before knowing
what the material looks like would guess at the shape of it. Once the first two
or three real jobs land, the collection schema, the route, the schema.org
markup and the hub get built around what the material actually is, and the rest
pour into that.

## What happens in the meantime

Phase 5 — the blue-ocean clusters — is not blocked. It is informational content
built from researchable facts about buildings, pests and this climate, which is
the same footing as the 23 problem pages already written. That work proceeds
while this sits waiting.
