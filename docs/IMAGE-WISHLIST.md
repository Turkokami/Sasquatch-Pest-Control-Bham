## Tier 1 — shoot these first

These are the pages that sell work, and they are the ones a competitor is
least likely to have real photographs of.

### The eight commercial verticals

Nothing in the archive covers these. Each needs one wide establishing shot on
a real site, ideally with a technician working in frame:

- `dairy-and-livestock` — a barn or milking parlor, bait stations along a wall
- `berry-and-food-processing` — a processing floor or cold-storage dock
- `restaurants-and-food-service` — a kitchen line or dish pit, after hours
- `multifamily-and-apartments` — a shared corridor, trash room or utility chase
- `schools-and-childcare` — an exterior, a mechanical room; **no children in frame**
- `healthcare-and-senior-living` — a service corridor or loading area
- `retail-and-grocery` — covered (scissor lift at a store canopy)
- `government-and-municipal` — a civic building exterior, ledge or roofline
- `marinas-and-waterfront` — dock lines, gear sheds, a bait locker

Access is the hard part here, not photography. One cooperative account per
vertical is enough, and a written OK from the property manager is worth having
before shooting.

## Tier 2 — the pest library

**Five of the 41 species pages now carry a photograph. The other 36 need one
taken, and that number is much worse than it first looked.**

Matching the archive loosely — "spider" for giant house spider, "ant" for
odorous house ant — found something for 32 of the 41. Every one of those would
have been a claim the photograph could not support, and on an identification
page that is worse than no photograph: the page teaches a reader how to tell
two species apart and then illustrates itself with the wrong one.

Requiring the archive's own description to name the species cut 32 to 11.
Looking at those 11 cut it to 5 — three were marketing graphics and two were
nests that cannot be separated from a close relative in a photograph. The
bald-faced hornet is the clearest case: a large gray enclosed aerial nest is
built by bald-faced hornets *and* by aerial yellowjackets.

Placed: `bumble-bee`, `earwig`, `wood-boring-weevil`, and evidence shots for
`anobiid-powderpost-beetle` and `carpenter-ant`.

**Nine species have nothing usable in the archive at all:**

`boxelder-bug` · `carpet-beetle` · `cluster-fly` · `honey-bee` ·
`millipede` · `pillbug` · `sawtoothed-grain-beetle` · `silverfish` ·
`western-conifer-seed-bug`

**The remaining 27 have nothing that names them.** These are the ones where a
loose match would have been tempting and wrong, and they include most of the
high-traffic species:

`aerial-yellowjacket` · `bed-bug` · `brown-marmorated-stink-bug` ·
`bumble-bee` · `carpenter-bee` · `cat-flea` · `deer-mouse` ·
`earwig` · `european-paper-wasp` · `house-mouse` · `indianmeal-moth` ·
`vole` · `webbing-clothes-moth` · `western-black-legged-tick` ·
`western-yellowjacket`

A species page needs one clear macro of the animal, in focus, on a plain
background, at an angle that shows the features the page says to identify it
by. A phone macro on a white sheet of paper is genuinely good enough.

Two of these are not realistically photographable on a job and should be left
alone rather than faked: `rodent-mite` and `western-black-legged-tick`.

## Tier 3 — the easiest wins on the site

The 23 problem pages each describe one specific situation, which means each one
already tells you what to photograph. Several of these you will pass on an
ordinary week:

- `crawlspace-vent-screening` — before and after on the same vent
- `sealing-a-quarter-inch-foundation-gap` — the gap, then the seal
- `rodents-in-the-attic-insulation` — tunneling in blown-in
- `rats-in-the-crawlspace` — runs along a vapor barrier
- `droppings-in-the-kitchen` — under-sink corner
- `sawdust-piles-below-the-trim` — frass cone on a sill
- `carpenter-ants-in-a-window-frame` — galleries in opened trim
- `moisture-ants-mean-a-water-problem` — damp sill plate with activity
- `wasp-nest-in-a-wall-void` — opened cavity
- `yellowjackets-in-the-lawn` — ground entrance
- `cluster-flies-in-upstairs-windows` — accumulation on a sill
- `fungus-gnats-in-houseplants` — sticky card in a pot
- `dampwood-termites-in-a-crawlspace` — damaged joist
- `soft-wood-around-a-window-sill` — probe in soft wood
- `bugs-on-the-south-wall-every-october` — mass on siding

**Before-and-after pairs are worth more than single shots** on all of these.
Nothing on the site currently has one, and it is the single most persuasive
format in this trade.

## How to shoot so it is usable

- **Landscape, not portrait**, wherever there is a choice. The site's reading
  column is wide and short; portrait crops to a letterbox.
- **Get a light source in.** Almost every rejected archive image was rejected
  for being too dark or blurred, not for its subject.
- **Include something for scale** in a damage shot — a pen, a coin, a gloved
  hand. A hole in a joist means nothing without it.
- **Shoot the same frame before and after.** Same position, same angle.
- **Full resolution.** Anything under 1,200px on the long side cannot be used
  at page width and is filtered out automatically.

## What not to send

The gallery filter drops anything the archive's own description says shows a
plate, a house number, a street address or a portrait — but it reads text, so
it cannot see what was never described. Please check for:

- House numbers, street signs, mail with an address visible
- Vehicle plates other than our own
- Customers, or anyone who has not agreed to appear
- Interiors identifiable enough that a neighbor would recognize the house

These photographs were already public on the old site, which lowers the stakes
without removing them: a customer's address on a linked gallery is a different
thing from the same photograph on a page nobody visited.
