# Rodent entry-point infographic — generation prompt and fact check

Written 3 Sep 2026, after a supplied AI infographic stated that mice enter
through holes "as small as a nickel or dime". That is wrong by roughly three
times, and this site's own blog post debunks it by name. Keep this file with
the prompt so the correct figures travel with it.

---

## The numbers, and nothing else is negotiable

| Claim | Correct | Wrong version in circulation |
|---|---|---|
| Mouse | **1/4 inch** — about a pencil | "a dime" (0.705 in) or "a nickel" (0.835 in) |
| Rat | slightly more than **1/2 inch**; an adult Norway rat wants nearer **3/4 inch**, the width of a **penny** (0.750 in) | "a quarter" (0.955 in) or "a half dollar" (1.205 in) |
| What to seal to | **1/4 inch for both species**, because a rat only needs that much of an edge to get its incisors on and chew the hole to fit | — |

Use **a pencil and a penny** as the physical comparison. Never a dime, nickel,
quarter or half dollar — those are the comparisons the site tells readers to
drop, and using them on our own graphic destroys the point.

---

## Read this before generating

**AI image models cannot spell reliably in dense layouts.** The supplied
graphic contained "seale" twice for "scale" and printed the same heading block
twice with different body text. A fourteen-label infographic will come back
with errors somewhere, every time.

So there are two routes, and the first is better:

**Route A — art only, text added afterwards (recommended).** Generate the house
illustration with numbered markers and NO other words. The labels are then real
text on the page, which means search engines can read them, screen readers can
read them, and a typo takes ten seconds to fix instead of a regeneration. This
is how the diagram already on the rodent page works.

**Route B — full infographic for social.** Fine for Facebook and Instagram,
where text in an image is normal. Proof-read every word against the table above
before it goes anywhere.

## Prompt — Route A (art only)

> Create a clean isometric cutaway illustration of a single-family Pacific
> Northwest house for a pest control infographic. No text, no words, no labels,
> no numbers anywhere in the image.
>
> The house: one and a half storeys, composition shingle gable roof, horizontal
> lap siding, a brick chimney, a detached garage, a covered porch, and a
> cutaway on the right side revealing a crawlspace under the floor and an attic
> under the roof. Around it: a lawn, one mature conifer with a limb reaching
> toward the roof, a stacked woodpile against a fence, a trash can, and a
> downspout running to the ground.
>
> Style: flat vector illustration, clean confident linework, subtle shading, no
> photorealism, no gradients on the background. Dark near-black background
> (#0a0806). Building and ground rendered in muted warm greys and browns. A
> single accent colour, neon lime green (#78ff31), used only for small circular
> numbered markers — draw 14 plain lime circles at the points listed below,
> with a thin lime leader line from each to the feature it points at. Leave the
> circles EMPTY; do not draw digits inside them.
>
> Marker positions, in order: 1 soffit where roof meets wall; 2 gable vent; 3
> chimney flashing; 4 tree limb over the roof; 5 gutter bracket and downspout;
> 6 a pipe penetration through the wall; 7 where siding meets the foundation; 8
> a low crawlspace vent; 9 a crawlspace access hatch at ground level; 10 bottom
> corner of the garage door; 11 threshold under the front door; 12 the woodpile
> against the fence; 13 a window air-conditioning unit in an upper window; 14
> the trash can and a spilled pet food bowl.
>
> Wide landscape format, roughly 16:9. Generous margin around the house so
> labels can be placed outside the image later.

## Prompt — Route B (complete infographic)

> Create a wide landscape infographic titled "Rodent-Proof Your Home: Where
> They Actually Get In", in a clean flat vector style on a dark near-black
> background (#0a0806), with neon lime green (#78ff31) as the single accent
> colour and warm gold (#ffbc00) used sparingly for the section headers. Body
> text in a clean geometric sans serif, white or light grey, high contrast.
>
> Centre: an isometric cutaway of a single-family Pacific Northwest house —
> composition shingle gable roof, lap siding, brick chimney, detached garage,
> covered porch, cutaway showing a crawlspace below and an attic above. Around
> it a lawn, a conifer with a limb over the roof, a woodpile against a fence, a
> trash can, and a downspout.
>
> Top left panel, headed "THE SIZE RULE": show a pencil beside a small hole and
> a US penny beside a slightly larger hole. Text exactly as follows and no
> other numbers: "A mouse needs about 1/4 inch — the width of a pencil." "A rat
> needs slightly more than 1/2 inch; an adult Norway rat wants nearer 3/4 inch —
> the width of a penny." "Seal to 1/4 inch for both. A rat only needs that much
> of an edge to chew the hole bigger." Do NOT use a dime, nickel, quarter or
> half dollar anywhere in the graphic.
>
> Fourteen numbered callouts in lime circles, connected by thin leader lines,
> grouped under four gold section headers:
>
> ROOF AND UPPER LEVELS — 1 Soffits and roofline, where the roof meets the wall
> and where two roof planes meet. 2 Gable and attic vents: a louvered vent with
> no screen behind it is an open door. 3 Chimney and flashing: the gap where the
> chimney passes the roofline. 4 Branches touching the roof, which are a bridge.
> 5 Gutters and downspouts, a climbing route.
>
> GROUND LEVEL AND FOUNDATION — 6 Utility penetrations: water, waste, electrical,
> cable and dryer vents, drilled oversize and never closed. 7 Where siding meets
> the foundation, the most common entry of all. 8 Crawlspace vents, whose screens
> corrode from the ground side. 9 Crawlspace access hatch, often just a board
> leaning against an opening.
>
> DOORS, WINDOWS AND GARAGES — 10 Garage door bottom corners, visible as daylight
> from inside. 11 Door thresholds with a worn sweep. 12 Window air conditioners
> left in after the weather cools.
>
> YARD AND HARBORAGE — 13 Woodpiles and vegetation against the wall: not an entry
> point but the reason there is pressure on one. 14 Food sources: uncovered
> trash, fallen fruit, pet food and bird feeders left out overnight.
>
> No watermark. No logo. Spell every word correctly.

## After it generates, check these five things

1. No dime, nickel, quarter or half dollar anywhere.
2. "1/4 inch" appears as the number to seal to, for both species.
3. Every word spelled correctly — read it out loud, including the section headers.
4. No duplicated heading or repeated block.
5. No generator watermark in a corner.
